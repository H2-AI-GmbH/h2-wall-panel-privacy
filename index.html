H2 Wall Panel

H2 Wall Panel ("the app") is developed by H2-AI GmbH, Germany. This privacy policy explains what the app does with data, what stays on your device and what leaves it.

Controller: H2-AI GmbH, Amselrain 43, 75038 Oberderdingen, Germany, Email: info@h2-ai.de

The short version: the panel sends its data only to servers that you set up and control yourself, to the web pages you open on the panel, and to Google Play for the purchase. The only thing that reaches us is what we need for the license: a pseudonymous device ID and, after a purchase, the Google Play purchase receipt. If you only use the free demo, the app sends the pseudonymous device ID to us once, for a check after installation, and nothing after that. There is no account, no analytics and no advertising.

Since version 1.1.0, you can enter a creator code before buying. To check it, the app sends only the code to our server, without any device ID (section 3 d), "Creator codes"). Business customers can also obtain licenses directly from us instead of through Google Play (section 3 d), "Licenses bought directly from us").

1. What we collect

We collect only the license data described in section 3 d): a pseudonymous device ID, the purchase receipt after you buy a license, a creator code if you enter one, and the technical server logs of our license server. If you write to us, for example with "Email support", we also receive your email (section 3 d), "Support requests by email"). Business customers who buy licenses directly from us also give us their order details (section 3 d), "Licenses bought directly from us"). The app contains no analytics, no crash reporting and no advertising libraries of any kind. Nothing from your dashboard, your broker, your camera or your settings is ever sent to us.

2. What stays on your device

The following is stored locally on the device and is never transmitted to us:

  • Your settings, including the dashboard address, screensaver options, schedule and orientation, stored with Android DataStore
  • Your exit PIN, stored as a salted hash (PBKDF2), never in readable form
  • Your Home Assistant access token, encrypted with AES-256-GCM using a key held in the Android keystore
  • Your MQTT broker password, encrypted the same way
  • For the automatic sign-in, a copy of the access token in the browser storage of your dashboard page on this device, which is where Home Assistant keeps any browser login. This storage belongs to the app and cannot be read by other apps
  • What the built-in browser keeps for the pages you open, such as cookies and page storage, as any browser does
  • If you use the photo slideshow, the permission to read the one folder you picked. The pictures are read from that folder and shown on the screen, nothing else
  • If you bought a license: the license for this device as a file signed by our server, so the full version keeps working offline, and until the license is confirmed, the Google Play purchase receipt. The license itself comes from our server, and the receipt is sent to it once, as described in section 3 d)
  • If you bought with a creator code: the code, until the purchase has been registered with our server. The code is sent to our server as described in section 3 d)

The encryption keys never leave the device's hardware-backed keystore. If the keystore is reset, for example by a factory reset or by restoring a backup to a different device, the stored credentials become unreadable and the app asks you to enter them again.

The app is excluded from Android's cloud backup and from device-to-device transfer, so none of this data is copied into a Google backup.

3. What the app sends, and where

The app contacts destinations that you choose yourself, Google Play, and, for the license, our license server:

a) Your dashboard, and pages you open from it

The app loads the web address you configure. That address is usually a Home Assistant instance on your own network. If you store an access token, the app passes it to that address so the dashboard logs you in, exactly as a browser would. The token is only ever handed to the exact address (scheme, host and port) you configured, and to no other site.

Links on your dashboard open inside the panel, as they would in a browser. A page you open that way is loaded directly from its own server, and that site's own privacy policy applies. If you switch on "Only dashboard addresses in the browser", links only open on your dashboard's address and on the extra addresses you list.

b) Your MQTT broker, if you switch that feature on

The home automation link is part of the full version. It is optional and off until you enter a broker and switch it on. When it is on, the app publishes the following information to the broker you entered:

  • Battery level and whether the device is charging
  • Whether the screen is currently showing something, its brightness, and whether the screensaver is on
  • Whether the kiosk is running and whether the kiosk lock is switched on
  • Whether the panel has a problem showing the dashboard, for example because it shows its offline page
  • Whether a scheduled dark period is running
  • Whether motion was detected, as a yes or no value
  • Wi-Fi signal strength
  • The address currently displayed, shortened to 250 characters
  • The app version and the device model
  • A device identifier made from the device model and the first eight characters of Android's device ID, and a panel name that you can change, so your dashboard can tell several panels apart

The app also subscribes to command topics on the same broker, so your dashboard can switch the screen, change brightness, start or stop the screensaver, reload the page, restart the kiosk screen, send the panel to a different address on your dashboard or on the list of addresses you allowed, and, unless you forbid it in the settings, switch the kiosk lock on or off.

This broker is yours. It normally runs on your own hardware inside your own network. We have no access to it and receive none of this information. You can switch the feature off at any time, and the app then publishes nothing.

c) Google Play

The in-app purchase is handled by Google Play, and the app may occasionally ask Google Play to show its rating dialog. See sections 8 and 9.

d) Our license server

The full version is sold as a license for one device. To bind the license to the panel and to check it, the app talks to our license server at lizenz.h2-ai.de, always over HTTPS.

When the app contacts it:

  • Once after the app is installed: when the app starts and no license is stored on the device, it asks our server whether this device has a license, so that a license bought for this device comes back without you having to do anything, for example after reinstalling the app. This check waits until the device is online. Once our server has answered (license found or no license), the app does not repeat this automatic check. If there was no answer, for example because the device was offline or our server could not be reached, the app tries again the next time it starts.
  • Right after you buy a license, to register the purchase. This also applies to a license you got with a promo code redeemed in the Google Play Store: the app registers it the next time it checks your purchases with Google Play. Such a purchase carries no device ID, so it is assigned to the first device of your Google account that registers it and does not have a license yet.
  • About once a day while a license is stored on the device and the device is online, to renew it. Without a connection, the full version keeps working for up to 30 days from the last successful check; after 20 days the app reminds you to connect.
  • When you tap "Restore license", for example if the automatic check could not reach our server.
  • When you enter a creator code and tap "Apply", to check the code. This request contains only the code and the name of the app, no device ID (see "Creator codes" below).

The app does not contact the license server in any other situation. If you never buy a license, the app sends the pseudonymous device ID to us only for the one automatic check after installation. Showing the device ID in the settings ("Show device ID") works on the device only and sends nothing.

What is sent:

  • Device ID: a SHA-256 hash of Android's device ID (ANDROID_ID) combined with a fixed value specific to this app. Our server never receives the Android ID itself. The value stays the same when you reinstall the app and changes after a factory reset. It is a different value from the MQTT device identifier in b).
  • Purchase receipt, only when a purchase is registered: the purchase token that Google Play gives the app, and the ID of the product bought.
  • The name of the app ("wallpanel").
  • Creator code, only if you entered one: when the code is checked, and again together with the purchase receipt when the purchase is registered.

When our server receives a purchase token, it asks Google Play to confirm the purchase and receives from Google the order number, the quantity, the purchase state, whether it was a test purchase, the device ID that the app passed to Google Play when you bought (section 8), the time of the purchase and, if you bought at a discount, which discount offer was used. It stores the device ID, the purchase token, the product ID, the order number, the quantity, whether it was a test purchase, whether the purchase has been consumed, and the time of registration. For each license it keeps a license ID, the device it is bound to and when it was bound, withdrawn or moved, and a log of these events. It then marks the purchase as consumed at Google Play. Our server also regularly fetches from Google Play the list of refunded purchases of this app (purchase token, order number, time, quantity, reason) and keeps it, so that a refunded purchase cannot become a license.

The license the server sends back contains the device ID, the license ID and how long it is valid, and is signed by our server. It is stored on your device (section 2).

Why, and on which legal basis:

  • To confirm that a purchase is genuine and bind it to the device it was bought on, and to renew and restore the license (performance of the contract, Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR).
  • To stop misuse: a purchase can only be registered once and only for the device it was bought on, and a license is withdrawn when Google Play reports that the purchase was refunded (legitimate interest in preventing fraud, Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).
  • To move a license to the same device after a factory reset, if you ask us to (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR).
  • To give a license back to the device it was bought for without you having to ask, through the automatic check after installation. If the device has a license, this serves the contract (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR). If it has none, the check is based on our legitimate interest in returning licenses to our customers automatically and in not offering a second purchase for a device that is already licensed (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). The answer "no license" is not stored on our server.
  • To check a creator code and to count a purchase made with it for the creator who handed out the code, so that we can pay the creator for the purchases made with their code (legitimate interest in working with creators and settling their commission, Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR; see "Creator codes" below).
  • Reading the Android device ID on your device is only done in the situations listed above, and when you tap "Show device ID" in the settings or "Email support". The license is bound to the device, and in our view recognising the device, including restoring a license bought for it after a reinstallation, is strictly necessary to provide the licensed service you use (Section 25(2) no. 2 of the German TDDDG). The device ID is used for nothing else.

Server logs: our license server receives your device's IP address with each request. The web server writes it to its access log together with the time, the requested address, the response code and the technical identification that Android sends with each request (Android version, device model and build number). The access log is deleted after at most 15 days. This also applies to the automatic check after installation; the access log does not contain the device ID. We use it only to operate the server and to fend off attacks; we do not use it to determine your location and do not combine it with the license data. To limit abuse, the server also counts requests per IP address for one minute, in memory only. The license service itself writes no access log and never logs device IDs, purchase tokens or order numbers.

How long:

  • Records of a license that can still be used are kept as long as the license exists.
  • Records of refunded or withdrawn licenses are deleted 12 months after the last withdrawal; they are kept that long so that the same purchase cannot be registered again.
  • Entries in the event log, and refunds reported by Google for purchases that were never registered, are deleted after 12 months.
  • A check that finds no license, such as the automatic check on a device without a license, is not stored in our database; only the access log entry remains.
  • The link between a purchase and the creator code it was made with is kept as long as the purchase record and deleted together with it.
  • Checking a creator code is not stored in our database; only the access log entry remains.
  • Licenses we grant directly to business customers (see "Licenses bought directly from us" below) are kept and deleted like licenses bought through Google Play.
  • Encrypted backups of the license database are kept for 30 days, server access logs for at most 15 days.

Deletion runs automatically once a day.

Where: the license server runs on a server rented from Hetzner Online GmbH in Germany, which processes the data on our behalf under a data processing agreement. Encrypted backups (GPG) are made every night and stored on our own file server, operated by H2-AI GmbH in Germany.

Creator codes: creators (for example video makers who present the app) can hand out a code that unlocks a discount at Google Play. Entering a code is optional. When you tap "Apply", the app sends the code and the name of the app to our server; our server only answers whether the code can be used and which discount offer belongs to it. This check sends no device ID and is not stored in our database. If you then buy, Google Play processes the purchase with that discount offer, and the app sends the code to our server together with the purchase receipt (see "What is sent" above). Our server counts the purchase for the creator only if Google Play confirms that it was made with the creator's discount offer. It stores which purchase was made with which code. You get your license whether or not the purchase is counted.

We use this only to settle the creator's commission. The creator receives from us only the number of purchases made with their code (including how many were refunded); never your device ID, order number or other information about you.

Licenses bought directly from us (business customers): business customers can obtain licenses directly from H2-AI GmbH instead of through Google Play. The customer sends us the device ID of each device (shown in the app's settings) and, if they wish, a name for each device. On our license server we then store, for each device, the device ID, a license bound to it, our invoice or order reference and the device name as a note. The device then receives its license in the usual way (see "When the app contacts it" above); no purchase in the app is needed. We use these data to provide the licenses and to move them to another device on request (performance of the contract, Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR).

The order itself (contact details, invoice and payment data) is not stored on the license server but in our accounting records. We keep these records as long as German commercial and tax law requires (Section 257 HGB, Section 147 AO; currently up to 10 years, for invoices 8 years) (legal obligation, Art. 6(1)(c) GDPR).

Support requests by email: if you tap "Email support" in the app (for example to move a license after a factory reset), the app opens your email app with a prepared message to info@h2-ai.de that contains the app name, the package name, the app version and the device ID of this device. Nothing is sent by the app itself: you decide whether to send the email and can change it before sending. If you write to us, we use your email address, your message and the details it contains (such as device ID and order number) to answer your request and, where requested, to move your license (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR, otherwise our legitimate interest in answering enquiries, Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). We delete the email once the request is settled, unless we have to keep it under commercial or tax law.

4. Camera and motion detection

Motion detection is optional and switched off until you turn it on. It uses the front camera, and only while the screensaver is running. It never runs while the dashboard is visible, and never while the screen has been switched off from your dashboard.

While it runs, each camera frame is reduced inside the app to 192 average brightness values, compared with the previous frame and then discarded immediately. No image is saved, no image is displayed, and no image is transmitted anywhere. Nothing derived from the camera leaves the device except a single yes or no motion value, and only if you have also switched on the home automation link.

While the camera is open, Android shows its green camera indicator. The app does not attempt to hide or suppress it.

Before the app asks Android for camera permission, it shows you a screen explaining exactly this, and you have to accept it. Declining does not affect any other function of the app.

5. Permissions

  • Accessibility service: used only to keep the kiosk locked while kiosk mode is running. It detects which app or system window comes to the front and puts the kiosk back, blocks the Home and Recents keys, counts Volume Up presses for the exit gesture, and closes the notification shade and the power menu, each only if that option is switched on. To tell the notification shade from the power menu, it looks at the titles and button labels of these system windows at the moment they open. Of other apps it only uses which app is in front. It does not read the content of your dashboard or of other apps, and it stores and transmits nothing.
  • Display over other apps: needed for the screensaver, the PIN dialog and, on Android 11 and older, the status bar block.
  • Usage access: used to check which app is in the foreground, as a second, independent path for the kiosk lock.
  • Camera: only for the optional motion detection described above.
  • Start at boot: so the panel comes back by itself after a power cut.
  • Keep awake: so the panel does not fall asleep on the wall.
  • Modify system settings: only requested if you choose a fixed orientation, and used only to set the system rotation lock.
  • Internet and network state: to load your dashboard, connect to your broker, and check the license.

These permissions are used solely to run the panel. None of them is used to collect data.

6. Background services

The app runs background services to keep the kiosk locked, to hold the connection to your broker, and, if you enabled it, to watch for motion while the screensaver runs. These services exist to keep the panel working unattended. Apart from the license requests in section 3 d), nothing is sent to us.

7. Transport security

Whether the connection to your dashboard and to your broker is encrypted depends on how you set them up. Home Assistant and MQTT brokers on a home network are frequently run without TLS, and the app supports that because otherwise it would not work on a typical home setup. TLS is supported and we recommend it. The app warns you when you enter an address that would send an access token unencrypted to a public host.

Because an unencrypted setup is possible, we state in the Google Play data safety section that data is not necessarily encrypted in transit. That refers to traffic inside your own network, between your tablet and your own server. The connection to our license server is always encrypted (HTTPS); the app refuses any other connection to it.

8. In-app purchases

The full version is unlocked through an in-app purchase processed by Google Play. Each purchase is a license for the one device it is bought on. We never see your payment details, your name or your Google account. When you buy, the app passes the pseudonymous device ID from section 3 d) to Google Play as part of the purchase, so the purchase can be linked to this device; Google Play may also use this value to detect irregular purchase activity. See Google's privacy policy at https://policies.google.com/privacy for how Google handles purchase and payment data.

9. Third-party services

The app uses two Google Play components: Google Play Billing for the in-app purchase, and the Google Play In-App Review API, which lets Google Play show its own rating dialog now and then. Both are run by Google under Google's privacy policy. Our license server uses the Google Play Developer API to confirm purchases and refunds (section 3 d). No other third-party service is integrated. There is no advertising SDK, no analytics SDK and no crash reporting SDK in the app.

10. Data deletion

To request deletion or a copy of the license data we hold, email info@h2-ai.de. Please give the Google Play order number (it starts with "GPA." and is in the receipt email from Google Play), the device ID, or both; for a license bought directly from us, our invoice or order reference. The app shows the device ID in its settings under "Device ID (for license support)": tap "Show device ID", then "Copy". We answer within one month.

  • With the order number (or our invoice or order reference), we delete the purchase with all its licenses, refunds, log entries and its link to a creator code. The license ends and cannot be restored.
  • With the device ID only, we end the license on that device and remove the device ID from all our records. The purchase itself stays recorded without the device ID, so that the same purchase cannot be registered again.

Copies in encrypted backups disappear within 30 days. Google Play keeps its own records of the purchase.

To remove everything the app has stored on the device, uninstall it, or clear its storage in Android's app settings. To remove what the app published to your own broker, delete the retained messages under the topic prefix h2wallpanel and the panel's entry under homeassistant/device on that broker.

11. Recipients and your rights

Recipients of the license data: Hetzner Online GmbH, Germany (hosting, as our processor), and Google (Google Play), which processes your purchase as the payment service and confirms purchases and refunds to our server. When you buy, we share the pseudonymous device ID with Google, which may also use it for its own purpose of detecting irregular purchase activity (section 8). Creators receive only the number of purchases made with their code, no personal data (section 3 d), "Creator codes"). For business customers who buy directly from us, a tax advisor (if we engage one) and our accounting software provider receive the order and invoice data as far as needed for accounting, and the payment service you choose processes your payment under its own privacy policy (section 3 d), "Licenses bought directly from us"). Nobody else. We do not sell data and do not share it for advertising.

You have the right to access, rectification, erasure, restriction of processing, data portability and to object to processing based on legitimate interest (Art. 15-18, 20, 21 GDPR). You also have the right to lodge a complaint with a data protection supervisory authority, for example the State Commissioner for Data Protection and Freedom of Information Baden-Württemberg.

Kaufen Sie nie eine Lizenz, sendet die App die pseudonyme Gerätekennung nur für die eine automatische Prüfung nach der Installation an uns. Das Anzeigen der Gerätekennung in den Einstellungen ("Show device ID") geschieht nur auf dem Gerät und sendet nichts.

Was gesendet wird:

  • Gerätekennung: ein SHA-256-Hashwert aus der Android-Geräte-ID (ANDROID_ID) und einem festen, app-eigenen Wert. Die Android-ID selbst erhält unser Server nie. Der Wert bleibt bei einer Neuinstallation gleich und ändert sich nach einem Werksreset. Er ist ein anderer Wert als die MQTT-Gerätekennung aus b).
  • Kaufbeleg, nur wenn ein Kauf registriert wird: das Kauf-Token, das Google Play der App übergibt, und die Kennung des gekauften Produkts.
  • Der Name der App ("wallpanel").
  • Creator-Code, nur wenn Sie einen eingegeben haben: bei der Prüfung des Codes und noch einmal zusammen mit dem Kaufbeleg, wenn der Kauf registriert wird.

Erhält unser Server ein Kauf-Token, lässt er sich den Kauf von Google Play bestätigen und erhält von Google die Bestellnummer, die Menge, den Kaufstatus, ob es ein Testkauf war, die Gerätekennung, die die App beim Kauf an Google Play übergeben hat (Abschnitt 8), den Zeitpunkt des Kaufs und, wenn Sie mit Rabatt gekauft haben, welches Rabattangebot genutzt wurde. Er speichert Gerätekennung, Kauf-Token, Produktkennung, Bestellnummer, Menge, ob es ein Testkauf war, ob der Kauf verbraucht ist, und den Zeitpunkt der Registrierung. Zu jeder Lizenz hält er eine Lizenzkennung, das gebundene Gerät und wann die Lizenz gebunden, entzogen oder umgezogen wurde, sowie ein Protokoll dieser Vorgänge. Danach meldet er den Kauf bei Google Play als verbraucht. Außerdem ruft unser Server regelmäßig bei Google Play die Liste der erstatteten Käufe dieser App ab (Kauf-Token, Bestellnummer, Zeitpunkt, Menge, Grund) und bewahrt sie auf, damit ein erstatteter Kauf nicht zur Lizenz werden kann.

Die Lizenz, die der Server zurückschickt, enthält die Gerätekennung, die Lizenzkennung und ihre Gültigkeit und ist von unserem Server signiert. Sie wird auf Ihrem Gerät gespeichert (Abschnitt 2).

4. Kamera und Bewegungserkennung

Die Bewegungserkennung ist optional und bleibt aus, bis Sie sie einschalten. Sie nutzt die Frontkamera, und zwar nur, während der Screensaver läuft. Sie läuft nie, während das Dashboard sichtbar ist, und nie, während der Bildschirm von Ihrem Dashboard aus ausgeschaltet wurde.

Während sie läuft, wird jedes Kamerabild in der App auf 192 mittlere Helligkeitswerte reduziert, mit dem vorherigen Bild verglichen und sofort verworfen. Es wird kein Bild gespeichert, kein Bild angezeigt und kein Bild übertragen. Aus der Kamera verlässt nichts das Gerät außer einem einzelnen Ja-oder-Nein-Wert für Bewegung, und auch der nur, wenn Sie zusätzlich die Anbindung an die Hausautomatisierung eingeschaltet haben.

Solange die Kamera geöffnet ist, zeigt Android seinen grünen Kamerapunkt. Die App versucht nicht, ihn zu verbergen oder zu unterdrücken.

Bevor die App Android nach der Kameraberechtigung fragt, zeigt sie Ihnen einen Bildschirm, der genau das erklärt, und Sie müssen zustimmen. Eine Ablehnung wirkt sich auf keine andere Funktion der App aus.

Ob die Verbindung zu Ihrem Dashboard und zu Ihrem Broker verschlüsselt ist, hängt davon ab, wie Sie beides eingerichtet haben. TLS wird unterstützt und von uns empfohlen. Die App warnt Sie, wenn Sie eine Adresse eintragen, bei der ein Zugriffstoken unverschlüsselt an einen öffentlich erreichbaren Server ginge.

Weil eine unverschlüsselte Einrichtung möglich ist, geben wir im Datensicherheitsabschnitt bei Google Play an, dass Daten nicht zwingend verschlüsselt übertragen werden. Gemeint ist Verkehr innerhalb Ihres eigenen Netzes, zwischen Ihrem Tablet und Ihrem eigenen Server. Die Verbindung zu unserem Lizenzserver ist immer verschlüsselt (HTTPS); eine andere Verbindung dorthin baut die App nicht auf.
